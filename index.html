How it works

In LTI terms, Cureum is the Platform and the partner product is the Tool. Cureum acts as an OpenID Provider: when a teacher clicks a partner resource inside a Skyline lesson, Cureum signs a JWT that says who the teacher is and which resource was clicked, and hands the browser to the partner. The partner verifies the signature against Cureum's public keys and shows the content.

Two things make this different from an ordinary LMS integration:

  • Teacher-first. Cureum is Skyline's learning-object repository. Launches come from teachers and curriculum staff. Student launches happen in Google Classroom, not Cureum, unless a partner and CPS agree otherwise.
  • Curation matters more than grading. The most valuable capability is Deep Linking: it lets the Skyline content team pick a specific article, book, or video from the partner's catalog and pin it to a lesson. Grade passback is out of scope for the first cohort.
Assumptions to confirm before the first partner kickoff. This process assumes Cureum exposes a platform-side LTI 1.3 implementation (issuer, OIDC auth endpoint, JWKS, token endpoint, admin screen for tool registrations, and a Deep Linking placement in the lesson editor). Confirm with Pepper which of these exist today, and whether Cureum will support Dynamic Registration. Phase 2 and Appendix B depend on the answers.

Meeting cadence: one 30-minute kickoff, one weekly 20-minute check-in per partner while in Phases 2–4, async in Jira otherwise.

Integration tiers

Partners declare a tier at intake. Tier 1 is the minimum to go live. Tier 2 is what makes the integration worth doing for Skyline.

Tier 1 · RequiredLTI 1.3 Core. Teacher launches a resource link (LtiResourceLinkRequest) with SSO. Partner maps returning users by iss + sub, never by name or email.
Tier 2 · RecommendedDeep Linking 2.0. Skyline staff open the partner's picker from the Cureum lesson editor, choose content, and the partner returns ltiResourceLink items that Cureum stores in the lesson.
Tier 3 · By agreementNames and Role Provisioning (NRPS) and Assignment and Grade Services (AGS). Only if student launches from Cureum are approved by CPS. Not in scope for the first cohort.

All tiers: HTTPS everywhere, RS256-signed JWTs, keys published via JWKS, and the OIDC third-party-initiated login flow from the 1EdTech Security Framework 1.0.

Two registration paths

  • Dynamic Registration (if Cureum supports it): Don sends the partner a one-time registration URL; the tool registers itself and Cureum issues the client_id. Fastest and least error-prone.
  • Manual (default until confirmed): Don enters the partner's values in the Cureum admin screen; the partner enters Cureum's values in their platform-registration screen. Both sides paste the final values into Appendix B and mark them as verified.
Register production only when Cureum's production issuer is final. The issuer is baked into every launch and every registration, so a domain change (for example, moving to the CPS vanity URL) means re-registering with every partner.
Gate 2 — Registered. Registration record complete for sandbox · both JWKS URLs reachable over HTTPS and return valid keysets · redirect URIs match character-for-character.

Smoke test, in order

  1. Login initiation. Cureum posts to the tool's login URL. Tool redirects to Cureum's authorization endpoint with state, nonce, login_hint, and the unaltered lti_message_hint.
  2. Launch. Cureum posts the id_token to the redirect URI. Tool validates: signature against Cureum JWKS, iss, aud equals client_id, nonce unused, exp/iat within five minutes.
  3. Claims. Tool confirms it received every required claim (Appendix C) and ignores any it does not recognize.
  4. Content. Tool renders the target resource in the placement Cureum requested (launch_presentation.document_target: iframe or window).
  5. Re-launch. Same teacher launches again; the tool recognizes the account by iss + sub and does not create a duplicate.
  6. Deep Linking (Tier 2). Content developer opens the picker from the lesson editor; tool returns a signed LtiDeepLinkingResponse; Cureum verifies it against the tool JWKS and stores the ltiResourceLink; the stored link launches correctly.

Use Appendix E when a step fails. Most first-launch failures are one of six known causes.

Gate 3 — Connected. Steps 1–5 pass for all three test accounts · Step 6 passes for Tier 2 partners · no manual URL editing was needed to make it work.

Phase 5 · Go live (Week 4–5)

  1. Register production. Repeat Phase 2 with production values. New client_id and deployment_id; production keys are never the sandbox keys.
  2. Production smoke test. One real CPS teacher account (signed in through RapidIdentity SSO) runs TC-01, TC-04, and TC-05 in a hidden lesson.
  3. Place content. Skyline content team deep-links the agreed resources into units. Lessons stay unpublished until placement is complete.
  4. Publish and announce. Lessons published; a one-paragraph note added to the Skyline Essentials onboarding materials: what the partner is, what teachers will see, where to get help.
  5. Watch for five school days. Don reviews launch errors daily with Pepper; partner watches their side. Rollback is a single switch: disable the deployment in Cureum. Resource cards then show "temporarily unavailable" instead of a broken launch.
Gate 5 — Live. Production launches succeed for five consecutive school days with an error rate under 1% · support paths tested end to end (Phase 6) · epic moved to Done.

Standing rules

  • Key rotation. Both sides rotate through JWKS with overlapping kids; the old key stays published for at least 24 hours. No email notice needed.
  • Endpoint changes. Changing a login, redirect, deep-linking, or JWKS URL requires 10 business days' notice and a re-run of TC-01 and TC-04 in sandbox first.
  • Annual re-verification. Every July, before the school year starts: re-run Appendix D in sandbox, refresh the privacy agreement check, and update contacts.
  • Offboarding. Disable the deployment in Cureum, remove resource links from lessons, and confirm the partner has deleted CPS user data per the privacy agreement.

Appendix E · Troubleshooting first launches

"Invalid redirect URI" or tool never receives the id_token
Redirect URI mismatch, usually a trailing slash or http vs https. Compare character by character against Appendix B.
"Invalid signature" / "key not found"
Tool cached an old JWKS or Cureum's kid changed. Tool should refetch JWKS on unknown kid. Check the JWKS URL returns Content-Type: application/json.
"Token expired" on a fresh launch
Clock skew. Both servers on NTP; tolerate up to 60 seconds.
Launch works in a new window but shows blank in the iframe
Third-party cookie blocking. Tool session cookies need SameSite=None; Secure; if the browser still blocks, tool should fall back to a "Open in new window" button rather than an empty frame.
"Missing lti_message_hint" or wrong resource launched
Tool altered or dropped lti_message_hint or login_hint in the authentication request. They must pass through unchanged.
Tool creates a new user every launch
Tool is keying on email or name. Key on iss + sub (plus deployment_id for the account).
Deep-linking response rejected by Cureum
Response JWT must be signed with the tool's private key (verifiable via the tool JWKS), carry aud = Cureum issuer, iss = client_id, and echo the deep_link_return_url and data from the request.
Roles not recognized
Tool expects short names ("Instructor"). Cureum sends full URIs per the spec; the tool must accept them.
